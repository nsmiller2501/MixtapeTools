# Health Production And Adaptation

**Summary**: The project's conceptual framework combines Grossman's health-capital model with Carleton et al.'s ex-ante/ex-post adaptation timing (source: Grossman_1972_JPE.pdf; source: Carleton_etal_2024_HESECC.pdf). Grossman supplies the health-production technology; Carleton et al. supply the timing of defensive choices under expected and realized environmental conditions (source: Grossman_1972_JPE.pdf; source: Carleton_etal_2024_HESECC.pdf).

**Sources**: Grossman_1972_JPE.pdf; Carleton_etal_2024_HESECC.pdf; Bento_Miller_Mookerjee_Severnini_2023_JEEM.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-05-19

## Core Mapping

Grossman models health as a durable stock $H$ that produces healthy time and can be increased through investment (source: Grossman_1972_JPE.pdf). Carleton et al. distinguish durable ex-ante adaptation $k$ from contemporaneous ex-post adaptation $b_t$ (source: Carleton_etal_2024_HESECC.pdf). The project combines these: $k$ and $b_t$ are defensive inputs into health production.

In the project, expected ozone $P_{im}$ is the environmental condition agents can learn about and adapt to before a daily realization, while $\epsilon_{it}$ is the transitory exposure shock (source: Carleton_etal_2024_HESECC.pdf). That makes the empirical ozone decomposition a health-production version of the climate adaptation framework.

## Interpretation Of The Coefficient Gap

The project interprets $\beta^S - \beta^N$ as the net health benefit of ex-ante defensive action. This is not because $k$ is directly observed. It is because the Grossman/Carleton framework says expected exposure should be partly absorbed through anticipatory health investment, while unexpected exposure has less opportunity to trigger durable preparation (source: Grossman_1972_JPE.pdf; source: Carleton_etal_2024_HESECC.pdf).

The key maintained assumption is timing: $P_{im}$ is known or learnable before the health-relevant exposure day, while $\epsilon_{it}$ is less anticipated except through forecasts or alerts (source: Carleton_etal_2024_HESECC.pdf).

## Why Alerts Matter

Alerts alter the information environment. In Grossman terms, they may change the perceived return to defensive health inputs. In Carleton et al.'s timing language, they may affect contemporaneous $b_t$ by warning about an incoming shock, and repeated alert regimes may also affect longer-run beliefs or investments $k$ (source: Grossman_1972_JPE.pdf; source: Carleton_etal_2024_HESECC.pdf).

This is why alert coefficients are not just policy effects layered on top of the norm/shock design. They help determine whether the "shock" is actually unexpected from the agent's point of view (source: Carleton_etal_2024_HESECC.pdf).

## Empirical calibration from Bento et al. (2023)

Bento et al. (2023) provide the empirical anchor for this framework in the ozone context. Their adaptation measure $\hat{\beta}_W - \hat{\beta}_C = 0.514$ ppb/°C quantifies the net reduction in the temperature–ozone response attributable to long-run behavioral and technological adjustments. This is the ozone-production analogue of the health-production adaptation $k$ in the Grossman/Carleton framework. The null OAD result ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{Shock} \times \text{Action Day}} = 0.068$, insignificant) provides a baseline: alerts do not meaningfully reduce ambient ozone concentrations nationally, suggesting any health benefit from alerts runs through individual protective behavior rather than aggregate pollution reductions (source: Bento_Miller_Mookerjee_Severnini_2023_JEEM.pdf).
